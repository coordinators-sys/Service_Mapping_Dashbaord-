What these figures mean

What the three states mean

  • Available — the service was present and running at the site when it was assessed.
  • Confirmed gap — the site was assessed and the service was not there.
  • Not reported — no partner has reported on this service at this site. This is not the same as a gap. We do not know.

What "coverage" does and does not mean

Coverage on this dashboard means a service is present. It does not measure whether the service is enough, whether it reaches everyone at the site, or how good it is. A site shown as having WASH may still have unmet WASH needs.

Where the data comes from

  • Reported by CCCM Cluster partners through the CCCM Cluster Service Mapping form on KoboToolbox.
  • Refreshed daily. This page shows data as of the date in the header.
  • Sites are identified by CCCM Site ID. Records that cannot yet be matched to a site are shown with a warning and excluded from matched-site figures.
  • Sector coverage is the share of assessed site-and-sector observations reporting the service as available. "Not reported" is excluded from that share and is shown separately.

What it does not cover

  • Sites not assessed in the current reporting round.
  • Service quality, adequacy, or accessibility for people with disabilities.
  • Service delivery not reported through the CCCM Cluster.

Matched by Site ID — the record is linked to an official site on the CCCM master list by its permanent Site ID (or exact name). Trust these; they feed the coverage figures directly.

Needs review — linked only by an approximate site name. Probably correct, but a person should confirm it. Still counted, and flagged for cleanup.

Unmatched — could not be linked to any master-list site. Usually the form carried no Site ID (or a code from a different tool), or the site is not yet registered in the master list. These are shown for transparency and excluded from master-list coverage counts.

"Unknown" sectors — a sector left blank on the form. It is never counted as "not covered"; it is excluded from the coverage-rate denominator so a blank can't understate coverage.
